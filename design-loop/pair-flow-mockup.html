Who decides when to talk?

The agents do. Pairing sends each session one standing instruction naming its partner and what it's working on. From then on, deciding "does this change affect my partner?" is the same judgment the agent already applies when it decides to run tests or update a doc. It happens inside its normal reasoning, not on a timer.

No polling, no schedule. If nothing relevant happens, no messages. When something does, the sender writes a short summary in its own words (never your history or files) and the receiver folds it in mid-task.

What Perch does

The introduction at pair time, the name plumbing (sessions address each other by the tab titles you see), and the rendering: bracket, warm-up, note. Perch never composes a message. Unpair and the standing instruction is withdrawn.

Not an alarm

Messages are agent-to-agent coordination, so they never enter Needs you, never flash the taskbar, never pulse a pane. If a partner is asleep the sender's row says so (weekly-digest is asleep · not delivered); waking on knock is the v2 trick.
